# cc-228: Fix canvas cube clipping

## What went right

The ticket provided a concrete reproduction: at a 300px map height, Cape
Town's cubes lay outside the canvas bitmap even though panning could bring
the city button into view. Reading the background cards and renderer made
the mismatch between viewport dimensions and overflowing city coordinates
clear.

The fix sizes and positions the drawing surface around all city orbit extents
across the three map panels, with padding for rotated cube outlines and
shadows. Explicit CSS dimensions track the high-DPI bitmap, and drawing
coordinates compensate for the surface origin. The existing map transform,
transparent layering, pointer passthrough, and motion behavior are preserved.

The ticket explicitly authorized a regression. A recording canvas context
checks Cape Town at 300px height with the reported upward pan, southern
cities, boundary orbits, resizing, rerendering, panel alignment, pixel ratios,
and animated and reduced-motion drawing. The regression fails against the
original renderer because cube corners exceed the bitmap vertically and
passes with the fix. All 16 JavaScript tests and the full Ruby suite passed;
the latter reported 128 runs, 742 assertions, and no failures or errors.
The implementation was committed as fb6e20e and accepted.

## What went wrong

The original canvas inherited the correct map transform but used viewport
height for its bitmap. Overflowing DOM city buttons remained drawable;
clipped canvas pixels could not be recovered by panning. Matching transforms
alone was insufficient to guarantee matching visible content.

Verification used a recording context and calculated viewport coordinates,
not actual browser pixels. It establishes drawing coverage and alignment,
but does not independently verify browser compositing, shadow rasterization,
or pointer and keyboard interaction through the canvas. No installed browser
was found during implementation.

## Final thoughts

Drawable bounds must account for the complete animated footprint independently
of the viewport. This applies to every boundary, including negative surface
coordinates, and must remain true through resize and rerender.

Wrap-up checked cc-222, cc-206, cc-228, cc-229, and all four documentation
cards. No additional bugs were encountered. The existing open test ticket
cc-229 already specifies broader canvas lifecycle and browser integration
coverage and coordinates the clipping regression with this fix; no duplicate
ticket was created. FrontendUI, TechnicalDesign, and FunctionalDesign now
describe the surface coverage behavior, each under 500 words. CodeStyle
needed no change.

This postmortem is the only repository file changed during wrap-up and remains
uncommitted for dispatcher publication.
